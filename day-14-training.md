## Learning Notes

Security principles are basic guidelines used to protect computer systems, networks, and information from threats. They help organizations maintain the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of their data.

### 1. CIA Triad

The CIA triad is a fundamental model of information security.

- **Confidentiality:** Ensures that sensitive information is accessible only to authorized users. Example: Using passwords and access permissions to protect private files.
- **Integrity:** Ensures that information remains accurate and is not modified without authorization. Example: Using file hashes to check whether a file has changed.
- **Availability:** Ensures that systems and information remain accessible when needed. Example: Maintaining backups to recover important files after a system failure.

### 2. Security Risk

A security risk is the possibility that a threat could exploit a vulnerability and cause harm to a system or its information.

Important risk-related terms include:

- **Threat:** Something that could cause harm, such as malware or phishing.
- **Vulnerability:** A weakness that could be exploited, such as outdated software.
- **Risk:** The potential impact and likelihood of a threat exploiting a vulnerability.

For example, an outdated application may contain a security weakness. If an attacker exploits it, confidential information could be exposed.

### 3. Security Controls

Security controls are safeguards used to reduce risks and protect systems.

There are three common categories:

1. **Preventive controls:** Help stop security incidents before they happen. Example: Strong passwords and access restrictions.
2. **Detective controls:** Help identify suspicious activities or security incidents. Example: System monitoring and security logs.
3. **Corrective controls:** Help restore systems or reduce damage after an incident. Example: Restoring data from backups and applying security updates.

### 4. Principle of Least Privilege

The principle of least privilege means giving users and applications only the permissions they need to perform their tasks.

This reduces the possibility of accidental damage and unauthorized access.

## Key Takeaways

- The CIA triad represents Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.
- A threat can exploit a vulnerability and create a security risk.
- Security controls help prevent, detect, and correct security problems.
- Backups support availability and recovery.
- Access permissions help protect confidentiality.
- The principle of least privilege reduces unnecessary access.

**Principle to remember:** Good security uses multiple layers of protection instead of depending on a single control.
