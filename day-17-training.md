## 1. Learning Notes

Today I learned about malware and phishing, two common cybersecurity threats.

**Malware** is malicious software designed to harm a device, disrupt its operation, steal information, or gain unauthorized access. Common types include:

- **Virus:** Malicious code that can attach itself to files or programs.
- **Worm:** Malware that can spread between systems.
- **Trojan:** Malicious software disguised as a legitimate application.
- **Ransomware:** Malware that locks or encrypts files and demands payment.
- **Spyware:** Software designed to secretly collect information.

**Phishing** is a social engineering technique in which attackers use fake emails, messages, or websites to trick people into sharing sensitive information or opening harmful attachments.

Examples of warning signs include:
- Unexpected messages asking for passwords or personal information.
- Suspicious links or unfamiliar website addresses.
- Attachments from unknown senders.
- Messages creating urgency or threatening account closure.
- Spelling mistakes or unusual sender addresses.

From my earlier networking and security lessons, I understand that protecting information requires more than technical tools. Users must also recognize suspicious activity and make careful decisions.

## 3. Key Takeaways

- Malware can damage systems, disrupt services, or compromise information.
- Phishing attempts often rely on urgency, fear, or curiosity.
- Unexpected links and attachments should be treated carefully.
- Software should be downloaded from trusted sources.
- Security updates and reliable protection help reduce risk.
- Never share passwords or sensitive information through suspicious links.

**Important lesson:** Think before clicking. Verify the sender and destination before trusting a message or attachment.
